# Home activity templates — parent guide and demo handoff

The three AI-assisted templates are authored by Jiabin and content-reviewed by
lychen, with status `reviewed`. Timings remain design targets, not measured play
durations. This guide is part of the activity: the short YAML
prompts alone do not contain the rules, story or clue answers needed to run it.

## Shared setup

Choose the age band and duration before preparing. Clear a small, dry floor area
and keep paths away from stairs, doors, glass and furniture corners. Walk indoors;
do not climb furniture. Paper marks a station: stand beside it, not on it.
Use loose, soft rolled socks without anything inside. Throw only at containers or
cup targets, never faces or bodies. Stop throwing before anybody retrieves socks.
Keep real doors unlocked; escape means completing a pretend control panel.
Pause for rest or water whenever needed. An adult stays involved throughout.

Preparation is outside play time: allow 5–10 minutes for either story activity
and about 3–5 minutes for the carnival. Set up only the materials used by the
chosen version. Keep clue cards visible and reachable; no searching in drawers,
cupboards, electrical areas or another person's belongings.

Each YAML has five stages per age band. All three families follow the existing
builder: **20 minutes uses stages 1–3; 40 minutes uses stages 1–5**. The two
story activities retain all five core tasks within their first three stages.
Parents give one subtask instruction at a time; a stage is not one long command
for the child to memorise. Both stories reach a complete ending in stage 3.
Stages 4–5 are additional episodes for 40 minutes. No duration exception is needed.
Carnival remains setup plus two games at 20 minutes, and four games at 40.

| Activity | 20-minute target, stages 1 / 2 / 3 | 40-minute target, stages 1 / 2 / 3 / 4 / 5 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Missing Power Crystals | 3 / 8 / 9 min | 3 / 8 / 9 / 10 / 10 min |
| Sock Ball Carnival | 4 / 8 / 8 min | 4 / 9 / 9 / 9 / 9 min |
| Secret Agent Escape | 8 / 7 / 5 min | 8 / 7 / 5 / 10 / 10 min |

Core task mapping (no task removed):

| Activity | Stage 1 | Stage 2 | Stage 3 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Crystals | Map and first crystal | Towel transport; astronaut and third-crystal rescue | Three loading deliveries; action code and launch |
| Escape | Badge recovery; route and code-card collection | Message delivery; number-action rehearsal | Final code ordering and escape |

Time windows include turns, retries, retrieval and transitions. They are design
targets, not measured or minimum exertion times. Pause or finish early as needed.

The last minute of the final stage is for the ending and shared tidy-up. Extend
a stage through its suggested new round or role swap, not by repeatedly reading
the same instruction. If a stage finishes early, the family may finish early.

## 1. Home: The Missing Power Crystals

Template: `activity_home_missing_power_crystals`.

### Parent preparation

For BOTH durations, use three rolled socks as crystals, a small towel, a soft
toy astronaut, paper and pencil. Draw circle, star, triangle and square station
cards, plus a ship/base. Place them beside clear walking routes. Draw a visible
map: circle → star → triangle (add square for ages 11–12). Keep crystal one
with you, crystal two at the final map station, and the toy and crystal three
aside until stage 2B. Prepare two paper loading-stop labels near the ship.

Opening: "Captain, our ship has landed on a strange planet. Three power
crystals are missing. We must find all three and rescue our astronaut before
we can fly home. Name our ship, then follow the first signal!"

### Stage 1 — Decode the planet map

Read: "Visit the planets in our map's order and report back to mission control."
Walk beside the marks, return and check the route together. Award crystal one
after exploring, without a speed or perfect-memory requirement.

- **5–7:** two or three visible stations; walk together first.
- **8–10:** three stations; look, cover the map, walk and check.
- **11–12:** four stations; plan the route and explain one choice.
Complete one exploration and a supported retry if needed in either version.
Keep map changes and role-swapped expeditions for stages 4–5.

Hint: point to the next shape or walk together.

### Stage 2 — Transport the second crystal and complete the rescue

Give instructions for A first, then introduce B once A is complete.

#### A. Towel transport

Read: "The second crystal is fragile. Our towel sling can carry it if we
coordinate our movements. Which clear route should we take?"
Hold opposite towel ends, carry crystal two together and park it at the ship.
If it falls, stop, replace it and resume. Walk, without pulling against each other.

- **5–7:** short straight route; child calls go/stop.
- **8–10:** choose a two-stop route and agree on turning signals.
- **11–12:** compare route options and explain which better protects the load.
Complete a short practice and one delivery in both versions.

#### B. Rescue the astronaut and third crystal

Place the toy and crystal three at different stations.
Read: "A signal from our astronaut! They have found the last crystal, but
need our help. How will we bring both back?"
Make separate trips for toy and crystal, returning each to the ship area.

- **5–7:** adult points to the two stations; child chooses which to visit first.
- **8–10:** child plans the two trips and tells mission control the order.
- **11–12:** child revises the plan if one imaginary route closes; another
  clear floor route must remain, with no furniture crossing or climbing.
Complete both pickups once in both versions. This rescue remains part of
the 20-minute experience; it has not become optional.

### Stage 3 — Load the ship, decode the launch sequence and fly home

Give instructions for A first, then introduce B after all deliveries.

#### A. Load the ship

Read: "Everyone is safe and all three crystals are here. We need to move
them through the loading stations and into the engine bay. You are in charge!"
Place the crystals at a nearby collection point. Child chooses the clear route
past two paper loading-stop labels to the ship. Move one crystal per trip and
check that all three arrive. The adult follows the child's directions on at
least one delivery; exchange roles on another.

- **5–7:** choose between two adult-proposed routes; adult helps count arrivals.
- **8–10:** choose loading-stop order and guide alternate deliveries.
- **11–12:** decide an efficient route, explain it and adjust unclear directions.
Complete all three loading deliveries in both versions.

#### B. Decode the launch sequence and fly home

Read: "Our ship needs an action password. Teach mission control your code,
check the crystals and lead the launch!"
Choose comfortable actions such as marching on the spot, reaching or side-steps.
Child teaches a sequence, both rehearse and perform it together after counting
all three crystals. The astronaut is aboard. The core story ends here; the
20-minute version is complete.

- **5–7:** choose two actions from adult examples, then copy each other.
- **8–10:** make three actions and teach the adult their order.
- **11–12:** make four actions; spot and correct an adult sequencing mistake.
Create one code, practise and launch in both versions. Teach one action at
a time for younger children before joining them into the sequence.

Closing: "All three crystals are loaded and our astronaut is safe. Engines
on — we're home! Tell me what we see through the window as we land."

### Stage 4 — A new rescue expedition: 40-minute extension

Read: "We are home, but a new rescue signal has arrived! Our astronaut needs
help on another planet. This time, you can change the map."
Reuse the toy and crystals. Move two station labels, plan a new clear route,
then alternate navigator and carrier. Try two rescue rounds and compare the
routes. Ages 5–7 choose between two layouts; ages 8–10 choose stop order;
ages 11–12 declare one imaginary route closed and revise the plan.

### Stage 5 — Child-led expedition and second landing: 40-minute extension

Child designs a loading route and a new launch code, using the same materials.
Test the expedition with the adult following directions. Swap roles, change one
unclear instruction and play again. Ages 5–7 dictate a picture route and two
actions; ages 8–10 use two loading stops and three actions; ages 11–12 add a
delivery problem and four actions. Close with another successful landing and
tidy up together. These are new rounds after the complete stage-3 ending.

## 2. Home: Sock Ball Carnival

Template: `activity_home_sock_ball_carnival`.

### Parent preparation

Prepare four to six loosely rolled socks, six paper cups, a laundry basket or
cardboard box, and paper for position/target marks. Choose a short clear rolling
lane and a gentle throwing direction away from breakable objects. Use marks
beside feet. For 40 minutes, reuse the same six cups for the two snow forts.

Read: "Welcome to our home carnival! We have bowling and basketball today."
For 40 minutes add: "Then we'll open the snow forts and curling rink."

### Stage 1 — Open the carnival

Agree where to stand, where socks may travel and the shared retrieval signal.
Try gentle rolls and basket throws, adjusting the distance so success is
possible. The adult demonstrates, then the child helps set the play area.
Choose cooperative scoring or friendly personal scores; nobody is eliminated.

### Stage 2 — Sock bowling

Arrange six cups. Each player rolls two socks, notes the cups knocked down,
then retrieves and resets. Play three rounds before changing the arrangement.
Try a triangle and a line; swap who arranges and who bowls. Reserve the final
round for a shared goal, such as clearing all pins using both players' turns.

- **5–7:** adult offers two nearby rolling spots; count knocked cups together.
- **8–10:** child arranges the pins and compares results across layouts.
- **11–12:** child tests two layouts over three rounds each and explains which
  needs more control. Adjust each player's distance to keep the game fair.

If too hard, shorten the distance or separate the pins; if too easy, change
their layout before increasing distance. Retrieve only after rolling stops.

### Stage 3 — Sock basketball: complete 20-minute ending

Take turns with three gentle tosses each, then collect together. Start close;
try a second spot when ready. Change one thing per round: position, throwing
hand if comfortable, or scoring rule. Finish with a shared goal chosen after
the practice round, such as making three baskets together with as many turns
as needed. This is a full carnival finish for the 20-minute version.

- **5–7:** basket nearby; adult counts shared successes, child chooses last spot.
- **8–10:** two spots and alternating rounds; choose an achievable team target.
- **11–12:** design a scoring rule and fair distances, then revise after testing.

Closing for 20 minutes: "Our carnival is complete! Which game shall we bring
back another day? Let's collect our equipment together."

### Stage 4 — Snow-fort showdown: 40-minute extension

Each player arranges three cups as a ground-level fort. Alternate one gentle
throw at the other fort from agreed spots. The non-thrower waits outside the
throwing lane. After each has thrown three times, stop, collect and rebuild.
Try a line, triangle or spaced pattern. End by combining the cups into one
fort and working together to knock them down. The cups are the only targets;
there is no throwing at people, chasing or dodging.

- **5–7:** adult helps build; use a close position and focus on aiming.
- **8–10:** child chooses a new arrangement each round and compares outcomes.
- **11–12:** predict which layout is harder to hit, test, then redesign.

### Stage 5 — Sock curling and carnival close

Lay a paper target at the end of the clear lane. Each player rolls three socks
to stop near it. Leave them in place until the round ends, compare distances
by eye, then retrieve together. Try a new target position and swap who coaches.
If socks do not roll well, reshape loosely or use a gentle underarm slide on
the floor; no hard objects go inside the socks.

- **5–7:** aim to finish near the paper; choose which sock landed closest.
- **8–10:** adjust force after comparing three attempts; try a joint target.
- **11–12:** predict stopping points, test and coach the adult using observations.

Finish with a shared last round and choose a favourite carnival event while
tidying up. No overall winner is necessary.

## 3. Home: Secret Agent Escape

Template: `activity_home_secret_agent_escape`.

### Parent preparation: 5–10 minutes

Make a base/control panel and three reachable, visible stations labelled
**STAR**, **CIRCLE**, **TRIANGLE**, with matching drawings. Put their symbols on
upright cards or beside the walking space. The game takes place in this small
designated area, never behind a locked door. You are mission control, not an
opponent trying to prevent the child finishing.

Copy these cards; choose the age-specific wording below before play:

| Card | Exact content or drawing | Where it starts |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Badge piece A | `1: STAR` plus a star drawing | CIRCLE station |
| Badge piece B | `2: CIRCLE` plus a circle drawing | TRIANGLE station |
| Badge piece C | `3: TRIANGLE` plus a triangle drawing | STAR station |
| Code card | `STAR = 2` | Adult-held envelope/pile labelled STAR |
| Code card | `CIRCLE = 4` | Adult-held envelope/pile labelled CIRCLE |
| Code card | `TRIANGLE = 1` | Adult-held envelope/pile labelled TRIANGLE |
| Final clue | `Read the numbers in your badge route order.` | Adult hands it over at CIRCLE after stage-2A delivery |
| Action key | `2 = two side-steps; 4 = four marching steps; 1 = one reach` | With adult until stage 2B |

For ages 11–12, replace the three numbered badge-piece contents with these
ordering clues: A = `CIRCLE comes immediately after STAR`; B = `TRIANGLE is
last`; C = `Visit each symbol once`. Keep the same hiding stations and code.
For ages 5–7 use drawings prominently; the adult reads and counts with the child.

On the reverse of piece A draw TRIANGLE; on the reverse of piece B draw STAR;
on piece C write/draw `Return to base`. Draw CIRCLE on a separate starting clue.
Only badge pieces are placed out initially; hand over code cards at stage 1B
so they cannot be collected prematurely. The backs provide the stage-1A search
trail; the fronts become the different stage-1B route. Explain when to turn them.

For BOTH durations, write a delivery message `Request the final access clue`.
At stage 2A, the adult exchanges this message for the final clue at CIRCLE.
For 40 minutes, also prepare two rolled socks as supplies, a small towel and
spare paper for additional delivery rounds and a child-designed replay.

### Stage 1 — Recover the badge and collect the access code

Give the two sets of instructions separately.

#### A. Recover the badge

Read: "Agent, our pretend training base is in protection mode. We need your
badge, an access code and an action password to reopen headquarters. You can
ask mission control for a hint at any time. First, follow this circle clue."

Child walks to CIRCLE, finds A, brings it back; its reverse sends them to
TRIANGLE for B, then STAR for C. Bring one piece to base each trip. Put the
pieces face-up and work out the route for part B of this stage.

- **5–7:** adult accompanies the first trip, points to pictures and helps order
  the numbered fronts. Walk the assembled route together as a rehearsal.
- **8–10:** child follows the trail independently within the designated area,
  orders the fronts and rehearses the route before turning the cards over.
- **11–12:** child solves the three ordering statements and explains the route.

Complete the entire search trail and assemble the badge in both versions.
Next, explain that the badge fronts reveal a different route for the code cards.

#### B. Collect the access code

Read: "Your badge has revealed the patrol route. Visit those stations in
order and bring their code cards to the control panel."

Correct route: **STAR → CIRCLE → TRIANGLE**. Hand over the matching code card
when the child reaches each station. They return it to base before visiting
the next station. A mistaken visit earns a hint and a retry, not a penalty.
Complete one collection patrol and check the full card set in both versions.
Badge recovery and code-card collection are both required at 20 minutes.

- **5–7:** route stays visible; adult helps match shapes and numbers.
- **8–10:** look, remember the next station, walk there and return to check.
- **11–12:** justify each station using the ordering clues; audit the adult's
  patrol and explain any correction.

### Stage 2 — Deliver the request and learn the action key

Give instructions for A, then teach B once the child returns to base.

#### A. Deliver the request and earn the final clue

Read: "We have the numbers, but need the final instruction. Send this request
to the correct station and bring back its reply."
Give the message and an age-specific destination clue:

- **5–7:** draw CIRCLE and say "Deliver here".
- **8–10:** `Deliver to the second station in your badge route`.
- **11–12:** show `Deliver to the second badge station` and the decoy `Deliver
  to TRIANGLE; it is the second badge station`. Verify against the route and
  reject the false claim. The established route is the trusted reference.

Answer: **CIRCLE**. Child delivers the message and the adult hands over the
final clue: `Read the numbers in your badge route order`. Return it to base.
If stuck, point to the badge's second symbol, then accompany the child.

Complete the message delivery and return in both versions. Keep extra supply
deliveries for stage 4; the final clue must be available for the 20-minute ending.

#### B. Learn the action key

Read: "Our lock uses both numbers and movements. Let's learn what each number
means before trying the complete password."
Show the action key: **2 = two side-steps; 4 = four marching steps on the spot;
1 = one comfortable reach**. Match code cards to actions and practise each
group in different orders; do not declare headquarters open yet.

- **5–7:** adult points and counts aloud; child copies each action group.
- **8–10:** child matches numbers to movements and teaches each group.
- **11–12:** child explains the mapping and corrects an adult rehearsal mistake.
Practise all three groups and exchange leader once in both versions. Teach
one group at a time; the full ordered password belongs to stage 3.

### Stage 3 — Solve the final code and escape

Read: "Now use the final clue to put our cards in order. Say the number code,
then lead us through its movements to open headquarters."
The badge route is **STAR → CIRCLE → TRIANGLE**. Corresponding code: **2–4–1**.
Perform two side-steps, four marching steps on the spot and one reach together.
The route and collected cards are both necessary to solve this last stage.

- **5–7:** adult helps arrange symbols and counts actions with the child.
- **8–10:** child orders cards and leads the complete action password.
- **11–12:** child explains the ordering evidence, then leads the password.
Solve, practise together if needed, unlock and close the core story in both
versions. All five original tasks are complete by the end of this stage.

Hint ladder: (1) "Which symbol comes first on your badge?" (2) point to STAR
and its number; (3) arrange STAR–CIRCLE–TRIANGLE and 2–4–1 together. Offer hints
freely, without a timer, lost life or penalty.

Core-story closing (finish here for 20 minutes): "Access restored! Headquarters is open. Your team
followed the clues and completed the password. Mission accomplished!"
The real door has remained open. Tidy the props together.

### Stage 4 — Rescue supply dispatch: 40-minute extension

Read: "Headquarters is open! Another team needs supplies. Can you plan a
delivery and send a reply?"
Reuse the trusted STAR → CIRCLE → TRIANGLE route. First send supplies to
CIRCLE: use its drawing for ages 5–7, "second route station" for ages 8–10,
and the same true/decoy instruction pair from stage 2A for ages 11–12.
Carry each of the two sock supplies there on a towel together, return to base,
then swap dispatcher/carrier. For a second round, target STAR: use its drawing,
"first route station", or a verified instruction plus the false claim
"TRIANGLE is the first route station" respectively. Let the child explain
or point out the right destination before moving. Finish with supplies delivered.

### Stage 5 — Child-designed practice escape: 40-minute extension

Child reorders the three stations to make a new practice route. Write the answer
first: for example CIRCLE → TRIANGLE → STAR means **4–1–2**, with the same
number-action key. Write/draw clues, walk the trail to check it, then let the
adult collect code cards and try the new password. Repair unclear clues and
replay that section. Ages 5–7 dictate pictures to the adult; ages 8–10 write a
simple station order; ages 11–12 add an unambiguous ordering clue. Close the
practice mission with its action password and tidy up. No additional props or
hidden locations are required.

## Integration and review notes

Validation after task consolidation: runtime YAML loading, structural checks
and content step checks passed for all three Home templates. All 18 combinations of
three templates, three age bands and two durations build using the existing
3/5-step policy. Each story's 20-minute output now includes the complete ending;
its detailed subtasks are retained in this guide. No duration-code change is
needed. The equipment allowlist now includes the required household materials.
The three Home templates pass full per-family content validation, including
review metadata. This does not establish that the entire legacy library passes
its checks. No family playtest or end-to-end app test has been verified here.

- All three YAML files live in `missions/reviewed/`, with `review.author: Jiabin`,
  `review.reviewed_by: lychen` and `review.status: reviewed`. No Home copies
  remain in `missions/_candidates/`.
- Duration policy remains unchanged: three steps at 20 minutes, five at 40.
  Each of the two story templates groups five core tasks into the first three
  steps; the guide supplies sequential subtask instructions for the parent.
- IDs all begin `activity_home_`; displayed titles all begin `Home:`. The prefix
  is a proposed routing convention, not functionality already implemented.
- Keep the existing fields: three age bands, five steps, `duration_bucket: 40`,
  `verify_mode: self`, `prompt_id: null`, `variable: true`. No runtime schema
  or service code is changed by this content delivery.
- `category: any`, `weather_tags: [any]` and `site_requirements: [bare_site]`
  are existing supported values. They do **not** enforce home-only selection.
  The backend must separate home templates from outdoor/place templates before
  activation. Adding their IDs to the demo allowlist alone is insufficient.
- The product requirement remains: always offer an At home option alongside
  place recommendations, including good-weather days. Venue-specific indoor
  locations receive no activity guide in the I3 demo. Neither routing change
  is implemented by adding these files.
- Real household equipment is listed explicitly and is supported by the runtime
  model and the content schema's equipment allowlist. The original outdoor
  equipment names remain allowed; arbitrary unlisted equipment remains invalid.
- The application currently transmits short steps, not this parent guide.
  For a facilitated demo, give the parent/facilitator this guide beforehand.
  For an in-app demo, provide a guide/preparation view or equivalent reviewed
  delivery mechanism first; the YAML alone is not a self-contained experience.
- `variable: true` preserves the current authored contract. Exact escape clue
  text, numbers, route and answers must remain fixed. Serve these missions from
  the library without model rewriting for the demo, or explicitly implement
  and validate protection for puzzle-dependent instructions before activation.
- Review the preparation burden, age suitability, clue clarity, available
  floor space and equipment; play through both lengths before claiming their
  duration. These templates do not establish 20/40 minutes of continuous exercise.
